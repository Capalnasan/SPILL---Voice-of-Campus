# Two-minute speech: Spill

Good morning.

Today I want to present Spill, a secure digital platform designed specifically for communication, feedback, and community discussion inside universities.

Universities are complex environments. Students and staff deal with unclear announcements, difficult administrative processes, problems with facilities, academic concerns, and questions that are often difficult to raise publicly. Spill creates one trusted space where these experiences can be shared, organized, and turned into useful information.

Access to Spill would be restricted to verified university email addresses. Anyone without an approved university email account would be unable to enter the platform. After login, each user would see only the posts, discussions, and university information connected to their own institution. This keeps conversations relevant and protects universities from unrelated or unauthorized access. Users could publish under a pseudonym, create posts, ask questions, vote on useful contributions, follow topics, receive notifications, and search discussions by faculty, course, campus location, or category.

Spill would include structured categories for teaching, exams, administration, infrastructure, safety, student life, and accessibility. Repeated reports could be grouped into trends, giving university teams a clearer view of problems that affect many people. Dashboards could show response times, unresolved issues, and common topics without revealing individual identities.

Safety and accountability would be built into the system. Users could report harassment, spam, misinformation, or privacy violations. Automated tools could help detect abusive language, duplicate posts, and urgent keywords, while trained human moderators would make final decisions. A transparent audit log, role-based permissions, rate limits, encryption, and carefully controlled identity access would protect the community.

De-anonymization would be an exceptional, formally documented process. It could begin only after an official certificate is issued by the recognized student association of that university—for example, COSPOL at UPT, OSUT at UVT, or SSMT at UMFT. The identity would then be revealed only when three separate digital keys are used together: the Rector’s key, the Head of the Ethics Commission’s key, and the key of a student member of the Administrative Council. If a post is approved for de-anonymization, the application would immediately pause, users would be informed that the process is about to happen, and the action would be recorded for audit.

Spill could also connect discussions to official university services. A post about a broken classroom could become a trackable maintenance request. A recurring question about exams could be answered by an authorized department. Users could see the status of an issue, who is responsible for responding, and what action was taken.

In parallel, Spill would generate a weekly report for the university’s Quality Assurance Commission. Each month, that commission would prepare a report for the Administrative Council covering recurring issues, responses, unresolved cases, and measures taken. These reports would then be published so the university community can see what action was taken and whether problems are being addressed.

Spill is needed because universities require more than occasional surveys. They need continuous, structured communication that helps people speak, helps staff understand patterns, and helps institutions respond visibly and fairly. By combining verified university-only access, university-specific content, pseudonymous participation, multi-key accountability, responsible moderation, and public reporting, Spill can make university life more transparent, responsive, and connected.

Thank you.
